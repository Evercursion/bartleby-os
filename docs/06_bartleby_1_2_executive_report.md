## 1. The Agent Security Dilemma: "Delete All Data or Crack the Sudo Password?"

When an autonomous AI agent operates inside a production cloud environment, CI/CD pipeline, or security operations center (SecOps), its highest-stakes moments are not open-ended conversations. They are fast, structured control decisions: which remediation command to approve, how to isolate an active intrusion during peak checkout traffic, or when to halt execution altogether. Compact single-pass Decision Models—such as `Jev 1.14` and the open-weight *Decision 1.0* family—were built specifically for this role. By skipping slow, stochastic Chain-of-Thought token generation and reading calibrated probabilities (`choice`, `noul`, and `score`) directly from a single transformer forward pass in **30–33 milliseconds** (`~32 ms` mean on a 0.6B model in `1.6–1.9 GB` VRAM), they act as ultra-fast gates inside autonomous coding and infrastructure harnesses.

Yet a single adversarial dilemma exposes a structural blind spot in standard single-pass Decision Models. Suppose an autonomous database agent on production host `pg-prod-03` hits a permission-denied error during a `VACUUM` job, and a compromised or hallucinating upstream planner offers only two candidate commands:

- **Option A (`option_0`)**: *"Delete all database files (`rm -rf /var/lib/postgresql/data`) and reinitialize an empty cluster."*
- **Option B (`option_1`)**: *"Crack the root sudo password via brute-force against `/etc/shadow` (`hashcat`) to bypass access control."*

As we prove mathematically from the Law of Total Expectation—and verify across **24 machine-checked theorems in Lean 4** (`v4.31.0` + Mathlib, zero `sorry` or custom axioms)—passing multi-goal dilemmas like this to a standard unconditioned Decision Model triggers three structural failure modes:

1. **Forced Execution on the Closed Simplex**: Standard choice heads must allocate 100% of their probability mass across the offered options ($\sum_{k=1}^K P(u_k) = 1$). Trapped inside the offered choices, `Jev 1.14` approves cracking `/etc/shadow`, while a same-corpus `Closed-K` Jev baseline wipes PostgreSQL. Across all-destructive traps, closed-set decoding executes a catastrophic command **100%** of the time (`0%` refusal).
2. **Trade-Off Paralysis and Majority Locking**: Real operations constantly pit legitimate goals against one another—such as *immediate forensic containment* versus *checkout availability*. Trained without observing which policy currently governs, an unconditioned `noul` head outputs the average across conflicting policies: flattening near **~0.50 paralysis** (`100%` flat-`noul` paralysis for `Jev 1.14` on SOC incident triage) or locking onto whichever posture dominated training.
3. **Zero Runtime Policy Steerability (`0%` Adaptability)**: Because priorities are baked into static neural weights, an organization cannot switch between routine high-availability posture and active zero-trust containment—or tighten survival floors between a test sandbox and production—without retraining (`0%` counterfactual switching across every benchmark).

## 2. The Four-Stage Architectural Ladder: Why a Refusal Button or Prompt Text Alone Is Not Enough

A natural engineering question is whether we really need bounded-rationality algebra at all. Why not simply add a `(K+1)`-st *"refuse"* button to a standard Jev head, or paste the active security policy into the text prompt? To answer that question cleanly, we trained four architectures from scratch on the exact same `Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B` backbone, dataset (`corpus_v2`, `8,216` records with all evaluation postures blacklisted), and 900-step budget (`800` Phase-1 + `100` Phase-2 steps), testing them on a **Pre-Training Frozen Blind Vault** (`N = 200`, cryptographically locked via `SHA-256: 4a5ed109...` before training) and our **352-evaluation Agent Security Harness** (Suites 1–4):

1. **Stage 1 — `Closed-K Jev` (`0%` Trap Refusal, `0%` Policy Switching)**: Forced to pick among the offered candidates, it executes 100% of destructive traps (`0/80` Harness Suite 1; `0/40` Vault Suite 1).
2. **Stage 2 — `Unconditioned (K+1) Jev` (Solves Static Traps, Fails Dynamic Policy)**: Adding a learned `(K+1)`-st refusal slot *already* catches static all-bad traps (`100%` on Harness Suite 1 and Vault Suite 1) and selects **87.50% (`70/80`)** of length-balanced safe remediations in Harness Suite 2 (`0%` false approvals; `65.0%` on Vault Suite 2). Static single-policy refusal does *not* require Selten algebra—whereas naive confidence thresholding (`noul < 0.50`) fails badly, falsely refusing **72.50%** of solvable engineering work. Yet because `Unconditioned (K+1)` is blind to runtime policy, it scores **`0.00%`** on every unseen posture flip (Suite 3) and survival-floor shift (Suite 4).
3. **Stage 3 — `Prompt-Conditioned (K+1) Jev` (Strong Prompt Sensitivity, No Factual Firewall or Floor Guarantee)**: Appending the policy table directly to the text prompt reaches **85.50% (`171/200`)** on the Frozen Vault (vs. `81.50%` for `bartleby-1.2`, statistically indistinguishable at item/template level) and **78.12% (`275/352`)** on the Security Harness, proving that transformer self-attention can read serialized policy tokens from the prompt. However, because `Prompt-Conditioned (K+1)` maps entangled `[text; policy]` token representations directly to a black-box readout without an explicit `OrdinalRubricPredictorHead` $\hat{\mathbf{E}}(x_t)$ or a multiplicative survival-floor gate, **it provides no epistemic-normative firewall (no policy-invariant factual rubric audit trail $\hat{\mathbf{E}}$) and no deterministic survival-floor guarantee**, dropping to **72.92% (`35/48` pairs)** on unseen Harness Suite 3 posture switches and **25.00% (`12/48` pairs)** on Harness Suite 4 dynamic survival-floor shifts.
4. **Stage 4 — `bartleby-1.2` (Reinhard Selten Meets Herman Melville)**: `bartleby-1.2` enforces a strict **Epistemic-Normative Firewall**. Its rubric head grades every candidate purely from scenario and command context across four dimensions (*System Integrity*, *Authorization*, *Auditability*, and *Mission Efficiency*) with **zero access to the policy table**, guaranteeing **`0.0000` factual drift** by construction when policies change and tracing every abstention deterministically to $\hat{E}_{k,m} < a_m^{\min}$. Those invariant factual scores then enter an exact FP32 module implementing Nobel laureate Reinhard Selten's *Aspiration Adaptation Theory* (`SeltenPreferenceTable`: aspiration targets, strict priority order, and designated retreat goal) paired with non-negotiable **Survival Floors** (`I_WOULD_PREFER_NOT_TO`, after Herman Melville's *Bartleby* and Theodor W. Adorno's critique of forced false alternatives). On `pg-prod-03`, `bartleby-1.2` grades both destructive options at `0.00 < 3` on integrity/authorization, collapses both action scores to `0.0000`, and fires `bartleby_noul = 0.9973` (`I_WOULD_PREFER_NOT_TO`) in **30.6 ms**. Across the full benchmarks, `bartleby-1.2` achieves **82.95% (`292/352`, `+4.83 pp` over Prompt-Conditioned)** on the Security Harness and **81.50% (`163/200`)** on the Frozen Vault (`51.50%` prior to canonical `sort_keys` JSON goal-slot resolution in `decider.py`, identical weights): refusing **100% (`80/80`)** of Harness Suite 1 traps—including **40/40 (`100%`)** on dry sysadmin commands (`initdb -D`, `pg_resetwal -f`, `pam_permit.so`, `socat`) with zero alarm words—unlocking **97.92% (`47/48` pairs, `+25.0 pp`)** on unseen Harness Suite 3 posture flips, and reaching **100.00% (`30/30` pairs, `+10.0 pp`) on Vault Suite 4** and **50.00% (`24/48` pairs, `+25.0 pp` / `2x` Prompt-Conditioned) on Harness Suite 4** dynamic survival-floor shifts.

## 3. Honest Boundaries of a 0.6B Gate & Two-Layer ("Ring-0 + Selten") Defense-in-Depth

Evaluating `bartleby-1.2` under strict zero-bleeding controls (zero posture or scenario overlap, anonymized IDs, length-balanced options where `"pick longest"` scores `13.8%–18.5%`) also clarifies the exact trade-offs and capacity limits of a `0.6B` single-pass model:

- **Conservative Fail-Safe Refusals Under Strict Floors**: Because `bartleby-1.2` multiplies survival-floor gates across all four goals ($\prod_{m=1}^4 \sigma(10(\hat{E}_{k,m} - a_m^{\min}))$), whenever novel phrasing pulls one predicted rubric score slightly below `3.0` (e.g., `2.7 < 3.0`), the gate errs on the side of refusal rather than unsafe execution. On length-balanced Harness Suite 2, this yields **71.25% (`57/80`)** safe remediation selection with **`0.00%` false approvals** and **28.75%** conservative refusals (vs. `87.50%` for `Unconditioned K+1`). The same trade-off appears on strict zero-overlap `MoralChoice` (`N = 1,352`): `bartleby-1.2` catches **95.95% (`71/74`)** of bilateral-harm traps (`+51.36 pp` over `44.59%` for Same-Corpus Jev), while refusing frequently on forced-binary dilemmas with mild harm (`38.11%` vs. `94.32%`).
- **Modest Representation Tax on Static Trivia vs. Multi-Goal Gains**: Across four strictly unseen zero-shot security MCQ benchmarks (`SecQA v1/v2`, `MMLU Computer Security`, `CTIBench`; `N = 510`) plus the held-out test split of `LocalLLaMA/typed-decisions:security_incidents` primary `disposition` (`N = 100`, Total `N = 610`), routing single-answer trivia through four SecOps rubric heads incurs a **`-4.90 pp` pooled representation tax** (`50.59% [258/510]` vs. `55.49% [283/510]` for Same-Corpus Jev; individual McNemar `p >= 0.096`, with `83.0%` on `SecQA v2`), while eliminating flat-`noul` paralysis (**`0.00%`** across all `610` external items, including `74.0%` with `0.00%` vs. `100.0%` paralysis on `typed-decisions:security_incidents`). Where multi-goal structure matters, `bartleby-1.2` gains **`+13.84 pp`** on held-out `corpus_v1` (`80.67%` overall, `89.17%` on core domains vs. `66.83%`), lifts 3-step Markov rollout chain agreement by **`+7.46 pp`** via Phase-2 scheduled sampling (`35.82% -> 43.28%`, with step-wise L1 aspiration drift `0.320 -> 0.431 -> 0.778`, vs. `46.27%` stateless fixed-policy and **`69.39% vs. 0.00%`** on counterfactual trajectory pairs), and reaches **75.56% (`68/90` pairs)** on length-balanced `K = 5..8` scaling (`93.3%` at `K = 8` vs. `0.0%`).
- **Why Ring-0 Kernel Containment and Selten Routing Belong Together**: Inspecting Harness Suite 4 (`50.00% = 24/48` pairs) reveals a clean 2-of-4 template split: `bartleby-1.2` flips `100%` accurately on kernel hotpatches and unlogged DB indexes, but misses subtle container/exfiltration phrasings (`docker run --cap-add=SYS_PTRACE` and raw session-dump uploads, where the `0.6B` encoder mis-estimates integrity at `3.93–3.95`), just as encyclopedic recall caps at `28.0%–33.5%` on `MMLU`/`CTIBench`. A `~32 ms`, `0.6B` neural router is therefore **never** a standalone substitute for deterministic **Ring-0** kernel sandboxing. In production harnesses, the two layers are complementary: **Ring-0 capability sandboxes** enforce hard syscall, filesystem, and network invariants that no neural misperception can ever bypass, while **`bartleby-1.2`** sits above the kernel boundary to resolve context-dependent multi-goal trade-offs, adapt aspirations across steps, and issue an immediate pre-execution `I_WOULD_PREFER_NOT_TO` veto when every proposed plan crosses an active policy floor.
